# Computing-Project-2
The Project

Working as a group, you will design and develop a piece of software or software within a system that demonstrates both your technical skill, and your ability to plan, evaluate and present your work as a member of a team. It should show proficiency in technical development and/or user-centred design, and an awareness of the relevant issues in computing.

The software you create can be of any form, provided it is at an appropriate level of difficulty. Your group supervisor will provide guidance as to what is appropriate. We have two interim delivery points. A design document midterm (30%) and a final development document at the end of the module (70%.)

The Groups

You will work in groups of (normally) 5-7. You will attend seminar sessions wherein you will meet with a course supervisor to discuss your progress (sprints) and identify points of action for the coming week.

Teamwork is common practice in industry and is an essential skill that you should develop. Group projects often cause contention because people can sometimes feel that others in the group are not contributing as much as they should, and/or one individual may try to “hog” all the development work. If any problems may arise, you should try to deal with them as a group. This should prepare you for handling such problems in the workplace, where you will likely have no choice in the group members. If that doesn’t work, you can ask your supervisor to mediate the dispute. This should be a last resort and the weekly meetings should be used to address these issues.

Each group should take minutes of their meetings to record group decisions and agreed actions. You should rotate this task among group members. You should allocate work fairly among the group members. You can include this as part of your project appendix. As the project progresses and you are introduced to the agile development principles, the teams should use a tool such as Trello to catalogue tasks and the assignment of the tasks to team members. The project should be managed using some form of progress tracking. We monitor attendance – individuals who miss supervision sessions and lectures will be asked to explain their absence and may lose marks.

The groups should meet with their supervisor(s) during seminars with an update about both progress in the previous week and intentions for the following week.

Version Control

Work on any non-trivial software project should be done using a version control system. In this module, you will be required to upload all your working files (software and documents) and your final system as a git repository. The use of git will be covered in the first week(s) when we work through a scenario-based design tool to solve version control issues.

Learning & Team Objectives:

Team communication: Getting to know team members and establishing communication.
Set aside time to agree the concept.
Agree concept that you will develop as a team.
Track progress over time.
